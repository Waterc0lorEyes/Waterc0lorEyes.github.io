跨文化沟通中的语境设定与信息传播速度 Contexting and Information Speed in Cross-Cultural Communication

2025-03-17 · 翻译实践

本文探讨了跨文化沟通中语境设定的多重功能与信息传播速度的谱系差异，包含英文原文、中文译文及逐条翻译注释。

原文与译文

原文

Cultural communications[1] are deeper and more complex than spoken or written messages. The essence of effective cross-cultural communication has more to do with releasing the right responses[2] than with sending the “right” messages. We offer here some conceptual tools to help our readers decipher the complex, unspoken rules[3] of each culture.

Almost everything in life can be placed somewhere along the fast/slow message-speed spectrum[4]. Such things as diplomacy, research, writing books, and creating art are accomplished in the slow mode. Buddha, Confucius, Shakespeare, Goethe, and Rembrandt all produced messages that human beings are still deciphering[5] hundreds of years after the fact. Language is a very slow message; after 4,000 years, human beings are just beginning to discover what language is all about. The same can be said of culture, which incorporates multiple styles of “languages” that only release messages to those who are willing to spend the time to understand them.

Contexting[6] performs multiple functions. For example, any shift in the level of context is a communication. The shift can be up the scale, indicating a warming of the relationship, or down the scale (lowering the context), communicating coolness or displeasure—signaling something has gone wrong with a relationship. In the United States the boss might communicate annoyance to an assistant when he shifts from the high-context, familiar form of address to the low-context, formal form of address. When this happens the boss is telling the subordinate in no uncertain terms[7] that she or he has stepped out of line and incurred disfavor. In Japan moving the direction[8] of the context is a source of daily feedback as to how things are going. The day starts with the use of honorifics[9], formal forms of address attached to each name. If things are going well the honorifics are dropped as the day progresses. First-naming in the United States is an artificial attempt at high-contexting; it tends to offend Europeans, who view the use of first names as acceptable only between close friends and family. With Europeans, one is always safe using a formal form of address, waiting for the other person to indicate when familiarity is acceptable.

Polychronic people feel that private space disrupts the flow of information by shutting people off from one another. In polychronic systems, appointments mean very little and may be shifted around even at the last minute to accommodate someone more important in an individual’s hierarchy of family, friends, or associates. Some polychronic people (such as Latin Americans and Arabs) give precedence to their large circle of family members over any business obligation. Polychronic people also have many close friends and good clients with whom they spend a great deal of time. The close links to clients or customers create a reciprocal feeling of obligation[10] and a mutual desire to be helpful.

译文

文化传播比口头或书面信息更为深刻复杂。有效的跨文化沟通本质不在于传递”正确”信息，而在于引发恰当回应。我们在此提供一些理论工具，帮助读者破译每种文化中复杂的潜规则。

生活中的几乎所有事物都分布于快/慢信息速度谱系上：外交、科研、著书立说、艺术创作均属慢速模式。佛陀、孔子、莎士比亚、歌德、伦勃朗留下的文化遗产，至今仍在被人类持续解读。语言本身就是一种极慢的信息载体——历经四千年，人类才刚开始触及语言本质。文化亦是如此，它包含多重”语言”体系，仅向愿意付出时间理解者释放信息。

语境设定具有多重功能。例如，语境层级的任何变化都构成信息传递：向高层级转变暗示关系升温；向低层级转变（降低语境）则传达疏离或不满，表明人际关系出现裂痕。在美国，当上司从高语境亲昵的称呼转为低语境正式称谓时，即明确传达对助理的不满。这种语境降级清晰表明下属行为失范已招致负面评价。在日本，语境方向调整构成日常关系反馈机制：晨间以姓名后缀敬语开启社交，若进展顺利，随着时间推移逐步省略敬语。美国人刻意使用名字直呼制造高语境的做法常令欧洲人反感，因为在欧洲文化中，直呼其名仅限密友家人。与欧洲人交往时，始终使用正式称谓最为稳妥，需待对方主动释放可建立亲密关系的信号。

多时性文化人群认为私人空间阻碍信息流动，割裂人际关系。在这类文化体系中，预约制度无足轻重，计划可能会在最后一刻调整，以优先满足个体在家庭、朋友或商业伙伴层级中更为重要的人的需求。部分多时性文化群体（如拉美和阿拉伯人）将庞大家族网络置于商业义务之上。这类人群通常拥有广泛亲密社交圈，投入大量时间维系客户关系。这种与客户的密切连结催生相互责任感，形成互惠义务网络。
