电刺激大脑能让人过度快乐吗？ Can Electrically Stimulating Your Brain Make You Too Happy?

2025-08-16 · 翻译作业

当医生可以直接接触患者大脑的奖赏网络时，一个深刻的伦理问题随之浮现：究竟应该让人感受到多大程度的快乐？本文翻译自《纽约时报》关于深部脑刺激技术的前沿报道，探讨了神经科学技术在治疗与增强之间的伦理边界。

电刺激大脑会让人过度快乐吗？

译文

Can Electrically Stimulating Your Brain Make You Too Happy?

原文

When doctors can directly access patients’ cerebral reward networks, someone has to decide just how good people should feel.

译文

当医生能够直接干预患者大脑的奖励网络时，就必须有人决定人们应该感受到多大程度的快乐。

原文

One man was pathologically addicted to gambling. Another was immobilized by a freak accident. Two women were prisoners inside their homes. Then the doctors turned on the electricity.

译文

一名男子病态地沉迷于赌博。另一人因一场意外事故而无法行动。两名女子被困在自己的家中。然后，医生们接通了电流。

原文

These are the stories of four patients who took part in a trial at the University of Oxford from 2018 to 2021, during which doctors implanted electrodes inside their brains to deliver carefully calibrated electric pulses. The treatment, known as deep brain stimulation (DBS), has been used for decades to treat the tremors, stiffness, and involuntary movements of Parkinson’s disease. But the Oxford team was using it differently.

译文

这是2018年至2021年间在牛津大学参与一项试验的四名患者的故事。试验期间，医生们在他们的大脑内植入电极，以释放经过精确校准的电脉冲。这种被称为深部脑刺激（DBS）的疗法已被使用数十年，用于治疗帕金森病的震颤、僵硬和不自主运动。但牛津团队的用法与此不同。

原文

They were not aiming at the brain circuits that govern movement. They were aiming at the reward network—a set of interconnected brain regions that govern pleasure and motivation, and which lie at the heart of severe depression, addiction, and obsessive-compulsive disorder.

译文

他们的目标不是支配运动的大脑回路。他们的目标是奖赏网络——一组互相关联的大脑区域，掌管着愉悦感和动机，也是重度抑郁、成瘾和强迫症的核心所在。

原文

For decades, DBS has been able to restore people to a functioning state. But as the technology improves, a startling question emerges: How good should people feel? And who gets to decide?

译文

几十年来，DBS已能够将人们恢复到可以正常生活的状态。但随着技术的进步，一个令人震惊的问题浮现出来：人们应该感受到多大程度的快乐？又由谁来做出这个决定？

原文

The question is perhaps the most profound one raised by the Oxford team’s trial. It is not only a scientific question. It is an ethical one, a philosophical one, a social one. It goes to the heart of what it means to provide medical care—whether the goal is to bring people back to “normal” or whether medicine should aim higher, offering what the trial’s lead researcher, Dr. Tipu Aziz, called “enhancement” as well as therapy.

译文

这个问题也许是牛津团队试验所提出的最深刻的问题。它不仅是科学问题，也是伦理问题、哲学问题、社会问题。它触及了医疗服务的核心意义——治疗的目标是将人们恢复到”正常”，还是医学应当追求更高目标，即如该试验的首席研究员提普·阿齐兹医生所言的”增强”而不仅仅是”治疗”。

原文

The tension between restoration and enhancement is only going to grow as brain-machine interfaces and other forms of neurotechnology advance. And the Oxford trial, while small, offers a vivid preview of the dilemmas ahead.

译文

随着脑机接口和其他神经技术的进步，恢复与增强之间的张力只会愈演愈烈。而牛津大学的这项试验——尽管规模不大——生动地预示了未来的困境。
